Technology comparisons usually collapse into advocacy. These are structured to resist that.

Every article here starts with the problem both options are trying to solve, because two tools only
compare meaningfully if they compete for the same job. Then the strengths and weaknesses of each,
the trade-off being made, the conditions under which each is the better answer, and the conditions
under which it is the worse one.

The recurring conclusion is that most of these questions have no context-free answer, and that the
useful skill is recognising which context you are in. An article that ends with "it depends" has
failed; an article that ends with "it depends on these three specific things, and here is how to
tell" has done its job.
